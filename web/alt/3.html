The problem

Making it is no longer the hard part. Looking at it is.

An assistant can produce a week's worth of material in an afternoon. What it hands back is usually a wall of text in a chat, and your feedback goes into that same chat, where it scrolls away.

Results in a chat

  • The numbers are buried in paragraphs, logs and JSON.
  • "Fix the third table" gets lost two messages later.
  • You correct a figure, and the next answer brings the old one back.
  • Nobody can see what changed between rounds, or who asked for it.

Results in Sliqtly

  • Every result is drawn: a chart, a table, a flow, a slide.
  • Comments sit on the slide they are about and stay open until resolved.
  • Your hand edits are kept, and the assistant is told what you changed.
  • Every save is a version you can compare and go back to.

The loop

Produce, see, review, steer. Then again.

The AI produces

Analyses, drafts, test results, plans and code reviews, written straight into a shared deck as Markdown.

You see it

The deck's theme lays it out; data becomes charts, steps become diagrams, long tables split across slides.

You review it

Comment on a slide, fix a number by hand, compare two versions side by side, or present it to the team.

You steer it

The assistant reads your open comments and edits as its task list, revises, and you check the new version.

↻ The next round starts from your comments, not from a new prompt.

See it

Drawn, not dumped.

The assistant writes plain Markdown with tables and a few lines of data. Sliqtly turns it into something you can judge at a glance.

Review it

Review on the result, not in the chat.

Comment on a slide

Point at the chart or the line you mean. The comment stays with that element and is open until someone resolves it.

Fix it by hand

Change a number, a word or a whole slide in the editor. Your edit is kept and reported to the assistant before its next change.

Compare versions

Every save is a version. See what the last round changed, and go back if it went the wrong way.

Review together

Share a link, present it in a meeting, or open the deck in a room where everyone sees the same slides and comments.

Steer it

Your comments are the next prompt.

When the assistant comes back to the deck, it reads the open comments and your hand edits first, works through them and resolves what it fixed. You look at the new version and decide whether to keep going.

Sliqtly connects to Claude, Claude Code and other assistants over MCP. That connection is just the cable; the point is what you see and decide on the other end.
